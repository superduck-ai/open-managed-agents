When self-hosting, the OMA control plane and your worker both process session data. Operators must configure access controls, networking and the runtime, and assess data flows through the control plane, model provider and external tools.

## What you own

* **Sandbox image quality and runtime hardening.** OMA does not inspect or verify your sandbox image. Follow best practices such as dropping unnecessary Linux capabilities, running as a non-root user, and using a read-only root filesystem.
* **Network egress controls.** Your sandbox's network access is determined by your VPC and firewall rules. Without egress restrictions, a compromised tool execution can reach arbitrary external hosts. Restrict outbound traffic to only the endpoints your tools require.
* **Service key storage and rotation.** The environment service key (`ANTHROPIC_ENVIRONMENT_KEY`) can access the environment's work queue, read its sessions, and read and submit their events. Store credentials securely, keep them out of sandbox images and logs, and follow your deployment's incident procedure if exposed.
* **Isolating untrusted workloads.** Configure workspaces, environments and worker permissions for the actual trust boundaries of your users and workloads; a separate environment alone does not imply single-user isolation.
* **Tool-execution blast radius.** Tools run inside your sandbox with whatever permissions your process has. Apply least privilege to the process user and mount only the directories your tools require.
* **Log retention and session content.** Conversation content and tool inputs and outputs may pass through the worker, OMA control plane and model provider. Manage and redact the data according to your deployment's data flows and retention policy.

## What OMA cannot do for you

* **Know that your key leaked.** If you suspect exposure, follow your deployment's incident procedure and verify that revocation and replacement have taken effect.
* **Verify your worker build.** OMA does not inspect your sandbox image or runtime. A supply-chain compromise in your image is not detectable from the control plane.
* **Isolate tools inside your sandbox.** OMA's security boundary stops at the sandbox. How you isolate individual tool executions from each other inside that boundary is entirely your responsibility.
* **Enforce data retention in your environment.** Once session content reaches your worker, it is outside OMA's data lifecycle controls.
